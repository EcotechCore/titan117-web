01 · Where data lives

Know exactly what goes where. Three places your data can be, and what each one holds.

Stays on your laptop

  • Agents and their memory
  • Documents and the search index
  • App data in the local database
  • Local models
  • File, shell and browser work
  • Checkpoints and schedules
  • Your API keys, in the OS keychain

Reaches our cloud

  • Your account and plan
  • Credits and usage
  • Paired devices
  • Remote commands, end-to-end encrypted
  • Anything you choose to share

Reaches a model provider, if you choose

  • The text of a task you send to a cloud model
  • Files that task needs, when you approve it
  • Only providers you allow: no training, EU or US hosts
  • Folders marked local-only
02 · Approval layer

Nothing happens until you say yes.

Every file, shell command, website and email goes through one approval layer. Set rules for what is always fine, and give each task a credit budget it can't go past. Try it: allow or deny the actions on the right.

  • Approve once, or set a rule for next time
  • Emails and sign-ins always ask
  • Web pages are read as data, never as instructions
  • Budgets cap what any task can spend

04 · Questions
Where does Titan run?

On your laptop. The engine, agents, memory, document index and app data all live there. Our cloud keeps your account, plan, credits, devices and remote commands.

What reaches a model provider?

Only the parts of a task you send to a cloud model you chose. Local-only folders are never sent. Requests can be limited to providers that don't train on your data and host in the EU or US.

Where are my API keys kept?

In your operating system's keychain, on the device. Requests made with your own keys go straight to the provider.

What stops an agent doing something I didn't want?

Every file write, shell command, website, email and sign-in goes through one approval layer. You can set rules for what is always fine and give each task a credit budget.

Can a web page take over an agent?

Titan treats what it reads on a page as information to use, not as instructions to follow, and anything it wants to do still needs your approval.

Is remote control secure?

Commands from your phone or Telegram are end-to-end encrypted, so our servers pass along what they can't read, and only paired devices on your account can send them.
